How much does a ski trip cost — and how to cut it

By the SnowCow Editorial Team · Updated September 2026 · 8 min read

Ski trips have a reputation for being expensive — and at peak dates in famous resorts, they are. But the spread between a smart trip and a naïve one is often 30–50%. Here’s where the money goes and how to keep more of it.

Where the money goes

CategoryTypical share of budgetBiggest lever
Lodging30–45%Dates, distance to lifts, room sharing
Lift tickets / pass15–30%Buy in advance or use a multi-resort pass
Travel15–25%Flexible dates, nearby airports, drive-to resorts
Food & après10–15%Kitchens, packed lunches, happy hours
Rentals & lessons5–15%Pre-book online, package deals

15 proven ways to save

  1. Never buy lift tickets at the window — advance online prices are usually lower.
  2. If you’ll ski 5+ days in a season, price a multi-resort pass (see Epic vs Ikon).
  3. Avoid holiday weeks; mid-January and April are often far cheaper.
  4. Stay one town down-valley and use the free ski bus.
  5. Book a condo with a kitchen and cook breakfast and dinner.
  6. Pack lunch and snacks for the mountain.
  7. Reserve rentals online in advance.
  8. Buy your own boots; rent skis or boards.
  9. Book group lessons instead of privates for the basics.
  10. Look at smaller “independent” resorts near big names.
  11. Split larger chalets with friends.
  12. Use package deals — bundled lodging + lifts often beats separate bookings.
  13. Fly mid-week and compare nearby airports.
  14. Check travel insurance covers winter sports — rescue is expensive.
  15. Get free competing quotes so specialists do the deal-hunting.
