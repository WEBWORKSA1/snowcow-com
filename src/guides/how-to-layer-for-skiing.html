How to layer for skiing & snowboarding

By the SnowCow Editorial Team · Updated September 2026 · 5 min read

Being cold ruins ski days faster than bad snow. The fix isn’t a thicker jacket — it’s layering: several thin layers that trap warm air, move sweat away and block wind and snow.

The three layers

1. Base layer — manage moisture

Merino wool or synthetic. Snug but not tight. Never cotton — it holds sweat and chills you.

2. Mid layer — insulate

Fleece, light down or synthetic puffy. This is the layer you add or remove as temperatures change.

3. Shell — protect

Waterproof and breathable jacket and pants. Look for 10,000 mm+ waterproofing (20,000 mm+ for wet climates), taped seams and vents.

What to wear by temperature

TemperatureBaseMidShell
Above 0°C / 32°F (spring)LightNone or thin fleeceShell, vents open
0 to −10°C / 32 to 14°FMidweightFleeceShell
−10 to −20°C / 14 to −4°FMidweightPuffyShell + neck gaiter
Below −20°C / −4°FHeavyweightFleece + puffyShell + face mask, mittens

Extremities matter most

  • Hands: mittens are warmer than gloves; carry liners.
  • Feet: one pair of thin ski socks — tight boots with thick socks cut circulation.
  • Head & face: helmet liner or balaclava, neck gaiter, goggles with no gap.
