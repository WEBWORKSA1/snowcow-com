When is the best time to go skiing?

By the SnowCow Editorial Team · Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

The “best” time depends on what you value most: deep snow, low prices, small crowds or sunshine. Here’s how the season typically plays out in each major region.

North America

The Rockies (Colorado, Utah, Wyoming) usually build their deepest bases from February to March. Coastal ranges (British Columbia, California) can receive huge storms but with heavier snow. Utah is famous for frequent, light powder storms mid-winter.

The Alps

High resorts and glaciers open earliest and stay snow-sure into spring. Watch out for school-holiday weeks in February, which vary by country and fill resorts quickly. Late March is a favourite for long sunny days on a deep base.

Japan

Hokkaido and Honshu’s Japan Alps receive frequent storms from late December through February thanks to cold air crossing the Sea of Japan — the source of the legendary light powder. January is usually the prime month for powder hunters.

Southern Hemisphere

Australia, New Zealand, Chile and Argentina ski from roughly June to October, with August often best. Perfect for northern skiers chasing a summer fix.
